the tabby

A tabby cat, or simply tabby, is any domestic cat (Felis catus) with a coat pattern distinguished by an M-shaped marking on its forehead, stripes by its eyes, cheeks, along its back, and around its legs and tail. Tabby cats display characteristic striped, dotted, lined, flecked, banded, or swirled patterns on the body: the neck, shoulders, sides, flanks, or chest. The four known distinct tabby patterns are mackerel, classic (or blotched), ticked, and spotted. Each is linked to specific genetics.

asian cat

Oddly enough, the Asian cat's origin is in Great Britain, not Asia. In the 1980s, crossbreeding between the Chinchilla Persian and the Burmese resulted in the emergence of a group of breeds collectively known as the Asian cat. This group included the Asian Tabby, the Solid-Colored Asian Cat, the Asian Smoke, the Burmilla, and the Tiffany. To achieve a solid-colored Asian cat, British breeders eliminated crossbreeding with the Chinchilla Persian. In parallel with British specialists, New Zealand developed the Mandalay breed, which closely resembled the solid-colored Asian Shorthair. According to international classification, the Mandalay breed is considered the same breed as the Solid-Colored Asian Cat. Since its recognition in the 1990s, this breed has become very popular and widespread worldwide, except for Russia.

Bengal cat

The Bengal cat breed arose from the deliberate crossing of a wild Asian cat with a domestic cat. The breed's founder, Jane Mill, an American biologist, wanted people to be able to keep an affectionate and tame animal reminiscent of a wild jungle cat, thereby preventing the extermination of this forest predator in its natural habitat. Bengal cats first competed in the TICA show in 1991, and today the breed is recognized by most feline organizations.

Sphynx

The Canadian Sphynx is one of several hairless cat breeds. During the breeding process, a natural recessive mutation was fixed , resulting in the absence of hair Currently, this is a fully formed and fairly stable breed with 50 years of history, transmitting its traits recessively. The breed is recognized by all international felinological organizations. Other hairless cats— the Don Sphynx , Peterbald , Ukrainian Levkoy , and others—are relatively young (around 20-30 years old) and are still developing.

black cat

A is a domestic cat with black fur. They may be a specific breed, or a common domestic cat of no particular breed. Most black cats have golden irises due to their high melanin pigment content. Black cats are the subject of mythology, legend, and superstition. They are often associated with witches and good or bad luck in European folklore. Any cat whose fur is a single color, including black, is known as a "solid" or "self". A "solid black" cat may be coal black, grayish black, or brownish black. Most solid-colored cats result from a recessive gene that suppresses the tabby pattern. Sometimes the tabby pattern is not completely suppressed; faint markings may appear in certain lights, even on a solid black cat. A cat having black fur with white roots is known as a "black smoke".
